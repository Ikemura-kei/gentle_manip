All baselines replace only the grasp-\emph{selection} step: each is executed by the same
unmodified controller, receives the same privileged object information (mesh, settled pose,
material parameters), and must pass the same geometric validity checks. Unless stated
otherwise the commanded width is the local cross-section at the chosen slice minus a fixed
margin, which reproduces close-until-contact execution. The comparison therefore isolates the
selection objective rather than the executor.

\paragraph{Naive top-down}
Places the gripper at the object centre with uniformly random yaw and closes to the global
cross-section along the closing axis. It encodes no contact geometry and establishes the floor
of the comparison.

\paragraph{Antipodal sampling}
Samples surface point pairs, retains those whose contact normals lie within the friction cone,
and ranks the survivors by cone margin, commanding the pair separation as the width. This is
the standard classical baseline and the candidate generator used by DefGraspSim
itself~\cite{defgraspsim}. We rank by cone margin rather than the Ferrari--Canny $\epsilon$
metric because $\epsilon$ is identically zero for a two-contact parallel jaw on smooth objects
of this class, and so cannot order candidates.

\paragraph{Geometry-only objective (\method{} w/o stress)}
Antipodal candidates are generated as above and re-ranked under the full geometric objective
employed by our planner---surface alignment, pad contact area, centre-of-mass lever arm, and
holdability---with the stress term removed. This baseline constitutes a leave-one-out ablation
of the selection objective: since all remaining terms, the candidate generator, and the
executor are held fixed, any difference in outcome is attributable to FEM-predicted stress
alone.

\paragraph{GPD}
The established classical planner of ten Pas \emph{et al.}~\cite{gpd}, which sweeps hand
candidates over the surface cloud and scores them with a CNN. We run the released
implementation on a dense privileged cloud, map its highest-scoring valid hand into our tool
frame, and command its own reported aperture as the width.

\paragraph{GraspNet-1Billion}
A modern learned 6-DoF planner~\cite{gn1b}, run as released with the authors' pretrained
checkpoint and no algorithmic modification. It predicts grasps from a single-view point cloud,
which we render from a steep virtual viewpoint; because the network regresses a rigid-object
pre-shape opening rather than a contact width, we convert its proposal using the same
close-until-contact rule applied to the other baselines.

\paragraph{Geometry-only poses with our closure}
Grasp poses are taken from the geometry-only objective and the commanded width from our FEM
surrogate. Comparing this variant against both the geometry-only baseline and the full method
decomposes the contribution of the stress model into the two decisions it informs, namely where
to place the contacts and how far to close.

\paragraph{Contact-GraspNet}
We also integrated Contact-GraspNet~\cite{cgn}, but its released implementation proved
unstable on our hardware: identical seeded inputs produced varying numbers of proposals or
aborted outright, a consequence of legacy custom kernels rather than of the planner itself. We
therefore report no numbers for it.
